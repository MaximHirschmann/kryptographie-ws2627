Question

Before HMAC was invented, it was quite common to define a MAC by $\mathsf{Mac}_k(m) = H^s(k \| m)$ where $H$ is a collision-resistant hash function. Show that this is not a secure MAC when $H$ is constructed via the Merkle-Damgård transform.

Merkle–Damgård outputs its internal chain value, so anyone who knows $H^s(x)$ can keep hashing more blocks without knowing $x$ (length extension). Write $\langle i \rangle$ for the $n$-bit encoding of $i$, $z_0 = IV$, and let $|k| = n$.

Attack

1.Ask for the tag of a one-block message $m \in \{0,1\}^n$. Since $k \| m$ has two blocks and length $2n$: $t = h^s\big(h^s(h^s(IV \| k) \| m) \,\|\, \langle 2n \rangle\big)$.
2.Output the message $m^* := m \| \langle 2n \rangle$ with the tag $t^* := h^s(t \| \langle 3n \rangle)$.

Why it is valid. $k \| m^*$ consists of the blocks $k, m, \langle 2n \rangle$ and has length $3n$, so

$$H^s(k \| m^*) = h^s\Big(h^s\big(h^s(h^s(IV \| k) \| m) \| \langle 2n \rangle\big) \,\Big\|\, \langle 3n \rangle\Big) = h^s(t \| \langle 3n \rangle) = t^*.$$

$m^* \ne m$ was never queried, so this is a forgery with probability $1$. Collision resistance does not help, because no collision is used. $\blacksquare$
