Question

Prove an analogue of Theorem 2.7 for the case of "almost perfect" secrecy. That is, let $\varepsilon \lt 1$ be a constant and say we only require that for any distribution over $\mathcal{M}$, any $m \in \mathcal{M}$, and any $c \in \mathcal{C}$:

$$\big|\Pr[M = m \mid C = c] - \Pr[M = m]\big| \lt \varepsilon.$$

Prove that the same lower bound on the size of the key space $\mathcal{K}$ relative to $\mathcal{M}$ holds for this notion as for perfect secrecy.

Claim. For every constant $\varepsilon \lt 1$, almost perfect secrecy still requires $|\mathcal{K}| \ge |\mathcal{M}|$ (script Theorem 6).

Proof (contrapositive)

Assume $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$. Fix $m_0$, a key $k_0$ and a ciphertext $c$ that $\mathsf{Enc}_{k_0}(m_0)$ can output. Only $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$ messages $\mathsf{Dec}_k(c)$ are possible decryptions of $c$, so some $\tilde m$ is never encrypted to $c$; clearly $\tilde m \ne m_0$.

Take $\Pr[M = m_0] = 1 - \varepsilon$ and $\Pr[M = \tilde m] = \varepsilon$. Then $\Pr[C = c] \gt 0$ but $\Pr[M = \tilde m \mid C = c] = 0$, so

$$\big|\Pr[M = \tilde m \mid C = c] - \Pr[M = \tilde m]\big| = \varepsilon \not\lt \varepsilon. \qquad \blacksquare$$

Unlike the script's proof, a uniform distribution would not suffice: it only gives a difference of $1/|\mathcal{M}|$, which may be smaller than $\varepsilon$.
