Question

Prove an analogue of Theorem 2.7 for the case of "almost perfect" secrecy. That is, let $\varepsilon \lt 1$ be a constant and say we only require that for any distribution over $\mathcal{M}$, any $m \in \mathcal{M}$, and any $c \in \mathcal{C}$:

$$\big|\Pr[M = m \mid C = c] - \Pr[M = m]\big| \lt \varepsilon.$$

Prove that the same lower bound on the size of the key space $\mathcal{K}$ relative to $\mathcal{M}$ holds for this notion as for perfect secrecy.

Claim. For every constant $\varepsilon \lt 1$, almost perfect secrecy implies $|\mathcal{K}| \ge |\mathcal{M}|$ (as for perfect secrecy, key-length theorem in the script).

Proof (contrapositive)

Assume $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$. Fix $m_0 \in \mathcal{M}$, $k_0 \in \mathcal{K}$ and a ciphertext $c$ that $\mathsf{Enc}_{k_0}(m_0)$ can output. The set $\mathcal{M}(c) = \{\mathsf{Dec}_k(c) \mid k \in \mathcal{K}\}$ has at most $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$ elements, so some $\tilde m \notin \mathcal{M}(c)$ exists. By correctness no key encrypts $\tilde m$ to $c$, and $\tilde m \ne m_0$ (since $\mathsf{Dec}_{k_0}(c) = m_0$).

Choose the distribution $\Pr[M = m_0] = 1 - \varepsilon$, $\Pr[M = \tilde m] = \varepsilon$. Then $\Pr[C = c] \gt 0$ (via $m_0, k_0$), but $\Pr[M = \tilde m \mid C = c] = 0$, so

$$\big|\Pr[M = \tilde m \mid C = c] - \Pr[M = \tilde m]\big| = \varepsilon \not\lt \varepsilon.$$

The scheme is not almost perfectly secret. $\blacksquare$

The uniform distribution (as in the script's proof) only gives a difference of $1/|\mathcal{M}|$, which may be below $\varepsilon$. Putting weight $\varepsilon$ on $\tilde m$ fixes that. For $\varepsilon \gt 1$ the condition would always hold.
