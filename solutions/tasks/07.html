Question

Assume that we require only that an encryption scheme $(\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Enc}, \mathsf{Dec})$ over a message space $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies the following: for all $m \in \mathcal{M}$, the probability that $\mathsf{Dec}_k(\mathsf{Enc}_k(m)) = m$ is at least $2^{-t}$. This probability is taken over the choice of $k$ as well as any randomness that may be used during encryption and decryption. Show that perfect secrecy as in Definition 2.1 is achievable with $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$ when $t \ge 1$. Can you prove a lower bound?

Hints: define an encryption scheme that fulfills the requirements; formulate a theorem that captures precisely what you are going to prove; prove your theorem while clearly reasoning from one step to the next.

Part 1: A scheme with $|\mathcal{K}| \lt |\mathcal{M}|$

Encrypt only the first $\ell$ bits with a one-time pad and let the receiver guess the remaining $t$ bits. Let $\mathcal{K} = \{0,1\}^\ell$ and $\mathcal{M} = \{0,1\}^{\ell + t}$; write $m = m' \| m''$ with $|m'| = \ell$, $|m''| = t$.

Gen$k \leftarrow \{0,1\}^\ell$ uniformly
Encoutput $c := m' \oplus k$
Decchoose $r \leftarrow \{0,1\}^t$; output $(c \oplus k) \,\|\, r$
Theorem. For $t \ge 1$ this scheme decrypts correctly with probability exactly $2^{-t}$, is perfectly secret, and has $|\mathcal{K}| = 2^\ell \lt 2^{\ell + t} = |\mathcal{M}|$.

Proof. Decryption returns $m' \| r$, which equals $m$ iff $r = m''$: probability $2^{-t}$. Perfect secrecy: the ciphertext is a one-time pad of $m'$, so $\Pr[C = c \mid M = m] = \Pr[K = m' \oplus c] = 2^{-\ell}$ for every $m$, which is the script's Definition 5 (equivalent to Definition 2.1). $\blacksquare$

Part 2: Lower bound

Theorem. If a scheme is perfectly secret and $\Pr[\mathsf{Dec}_K(\mathsf{Enc}_K(m)) = m] \ge 2^{-t}$ for all $m$, then $|\mathcal{K}| \ge 2^{-t} \cdot |\mathcal{M}|$. (Part 1 shows this is tight.)

Proof. Let $M$ be uniform over $\mathcal{M}$ and consider an eavesdropper that sees $c$ and tries to guess $M$:

  • Upper bound. By perfect secrecy, $c$ tells nothing about $M$, so any guess is right with probability at most $\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}$.
  • A concrete strategy. Pick a uniform key $k' \leftarrow \mathcal{K}$ and output $\mathsf{Dec}_{k'}(c)$. With probability $\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}|}$, $k'$ is the real key, and then the output is $M$ with probability at least $2^{-t}$. So this strategy succeeds with probability at least $\frac{2^{-t}}{|\mathcal{K}|}$.
$$\frac{2^{-t}}{|\mathcal{K}|} \le \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\mathcal{K}| \ge 2^{-t} \cdot |\mathcal{M}|. \qquad \blacksquare$$

For $t = 0$ this is the usual bound $|\mathcal{K}| \ge |\mathcal{M}|$ (script Theorem 6).
