Question

Consider a modified substitution-permutation network where the cipher instead first applies $r$ rounds of key-mixing, then carries out $r$ rounds of substitution, and finally applies $r$ permutations. Analyze the security of this construction.

Answer. The construction is equivalent to a single round of an SPN, so it is completely insecure.
  • $r$ rounds of key mixing are one XOR with $K := k_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus k_r$.
  • $r$ rounds of substitution are one fixed, public, invertible function $S$ (the S-boxes do not use the key).
  • $r$ permutations are one fixed public permutation $P$.
$$y = P\big(S(x \oplus K)\big).$$

Attack. From one known pair $(x, y)$, compute $K = x \oplus S^{-1}(P^{-1}(y))$. $K$ is all the key material that matters, so the attacker can now decrypt everything. $\blacksquare$

An SPN is secure only because key mixing, substitution and permutation alternate: a key added after a non-linear S-box cannot be moved back to the start.
