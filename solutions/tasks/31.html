Question

Let $(\mathsf{Gen}, H)$ be a collision-resistant hash function. Is $(\mathsf{Gen}, \bar H)$ with $\bar H^s(x) := H^s(H^s(x))$ necessarily collision resistant?

Answer. Yes. Any collision for $\bar H$ contains a collision for $H$.

Proof

Let $x \ne x'$ with $H^s(H^s(x)) = H^s(H^s(x'))$. Two cases:

  1. $H^s(x) = H^s(x')$: then $(x, x')$ is a collision for $H$.
  2. $H^s(x) \ne H^s(x')$: then $y := H^s(x)$ and $y' := H^s(x')$ are different inputs with $H^s(y) = H^s(y')$, a collision for $H$.

So an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ against $H$ runs a collision finder $\bar{\mathcal{A}}$ for $\bar H$ on the same key $s$, and outputs $(x, x')$ or $(H^s(x), H^s(x'))$ according to the case. It succeeds whenever $\bar{\mathcal{A}}$ does, so $\bar{\mathcal{A}}$'s success probability is negligible. $\blacksquare$
