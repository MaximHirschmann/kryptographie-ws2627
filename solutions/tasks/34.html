Question

Consider the following fixed-length MAC for messages of length $\ell(n) = 2n - 2$ using a pseudorandom function $F$: On input a message $m_0 \| m_1$ with $|m_0| = |m_1| = n - 1$ and key $k \in \{0,1\}^n$, algorithm $\mathsf{Mac}$ outputs $t = F_k(0 \| m_0) \,\|\, F_k(1 \| m_1)$. Algorithm $\mathsf{Vrfy}$ is defined in the natural way. Is $(\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Mac}, \mathsf{Vrfy})$ existentially unforgeable under a chosen-message attack? Prove your answer.

Answer. No. The two halves are authenticated independently, so halves from different messages can be combined ("mix and match").

Attack

1.Query $m_0 \| m_1$ and receive $t = F_k(0 \| m_0) \,\|\, F_k(1 \| m_1) =: t_0 \| t_1$.
2.Query $m'_0 \| m'_1$ with $m'_0 \ne m_0$ and $m'_1 \ne m_1$, and receive $t'_0 \| t'_1$.
3.Output the forgery $(m_0 \| m'_1,\ t_0 \| t'_1)$.

Why it works

$\mathsf{Mac}_k(m_0 \| m'_1) = F_k(0 \| m_0) \,\|\, F_k(1 \| m'_1) = t_0 \| t'_1$, so the tag is valid. The message $m_0 \| m'_1$ differs from both queried messages: from the first in the second half, and from the second in the first half. So it is a new message, and the forgery succeeds with probability $1$:

$$\Pr[\mathsf{Mac\text{-}forge}_{\mathcal{A},\Pi}(n) = 1] = 1. \qquad \blacksquare$$

The prefixes $0$/$1$ only prevent swapping the two halves of one message. They do not bind the halves together. A secure fix is to MAC the whole message with one PRF call, $F_k(m_0 \| m_1)$, or to use CBC-MAC.
