Question
  1. Show that the CBC, OFB and CTR-Mode are not CCA-secure encryption schemes (for every $F$).
  2. Do the operation modes presented in the lecture protect the integrity of the data? Discuss!

1. CBC, OFB and CTR are not CCA-secure

In the CCA game, the adversary may ask the decryption oracle about any ciphertext except the challenge itself. All three modes are malleable: changing one bit of the ciphertext changes the plaintext in a predictable way. So the attacker modifies the challenge slightly, has it decrypted, and undoes the change.

Use single-block messages $m_0 = 0^n$ and $m_1 = 1^n$, and let $\delta := 0^{n-1}1$. The challenge is $\langle c_0, c_1 \rangle$, where $c_0$ is the $IV$ or counter.

ModeDecryption queryAnswer
CBC$\langle c_0 \oplus \delta,\ c_1 \rangle$$F_k^{-1}(c_1) \oplus c_0 \oplus \delta = m_b \oplus \delta$
OFB$\langle c_0,\ c_1 \oplus \delta \rangle$$c_1 \oplus \delta \oplus F_k(c_0) = m_b \oplus \delta$
CTR$\langle c_0,\ c_1 \oplus \delta \rangle$$c_1 \oplus \delta \oplus F_k(c_0 + 1) = m_b \oplus \delta$

In each case the query differs from the challenge, so it is allowed. The attacker computes $m_b = \text{answer} \oplus \delta$ and outputs $b$. It wins with probability $1$. The attack uses no property of $F$, so it works for every $F$, even a truly random permutation. $\blacksquare$

2. Integrity

No. CBC, OFB and CTR can provide CPA confidentiality with the appropriate primitive and IV/nonce rules. ECB is not even secure for general multi-block messages against eavesdropping. They do not protect integrity:

  • Every string decrypts. Decryption never rejects, so the receiver cannot tell a forged or modified ciphertext from a real one.
  • Bit flipping (OFB, CTR): flipping bit $j$ of $c_i$ flips exactly bit $j$ of $m_i$. If the attacker knows part of the plaintext, they can change it to anything, e.g. "pay 100" becomes "pay 900".
  • CBC: flipping bits in the $IV$ flips the same bits in $m_1$. Changing $c_i$ garbles $m_i$ but flips chosen bits of $m_{i+1}$. Blocks can also be cut off at the end, which leaves a valid shorter plaintext.
  • ECB: blocks can be swapped, repeated or removed without being detected.

Integrity needs a separate mechanism: a MAC, ideally as encrypt-then-MAC, or an authenticated mode such as GCM. The CCA attacks above are exactly integrity attacks: the decryption oracle accepts modified ciphertexts.
