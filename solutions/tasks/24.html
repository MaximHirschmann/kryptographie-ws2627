Question
  1. Show that the CBC, OFB and CTR-Mode are not CCA-secure encryption schemes (for every $F$).
  2. Do the operation modes presented in the lecture protect the integrity of the data? Discuss!

1. CBC, OFB and CTR are not CCA-secure

All three modes are malleable: flipping a ciphertext bit flips a predictable plaintext bit. The attacker modifies the challenge, asks for its decryption (allowed, since it is a different ciphertext), and undoes the change.

Use one-block messages $m_0 = 0^n$, $m_1 = 1^n$ and $\delta := 0^{n-1}1$. The challenge is $\langle c_0, c_1 \rangle$, where $c_0$ is the IV or counter.

ModeDecryption queryAnswer
CBC$\langle c_0 \oplus \delta,\ c_1 \rangle$$F_k^{-1}(c_1) \oplus c_0 \oplus \delta = m_b \oplus \delta$
OFB$\langle c_0,\ c_1 \oplus \delta \rangle$$m_b \oplus \delta$
CTR$\langle c_0,\ c_1 \oplus \delta \rangle$$m_b \oplus \delta$

The attacker XORs the answer with $\delta$, obtains $m_b$ and wins with probability $1$, for every $F$. $\blacksquare$

2. Integrity

No. Every string decrypts to something, so the receiver cannot detect modifications. In OFB and CTR, flipping bit $j$ of $c_i$ flips bit $j$ of $m_i$; in CBC, flipping IV bits flips the same bits of $m_1$; in ECB, blocks can be reordered or removed. Integrity needs a MAC (e.g. encrypt-then-MAC) or an authenticated mode.
