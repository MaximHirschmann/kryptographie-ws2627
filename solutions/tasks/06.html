Question

Prove that we may assume that the key-generation algorithm $\mathsf{Gen}$ always chooses a key uniformly from the key space $\mathcal{K}$. In other words, for any distribution over $\mathcal{K}$ we can create another key space $\mathcal{K}'$ such that if $\mathsf{Gen}$ samples it uniformly, the distribution of keys is the same.

Hint: the probabilities with which keys are chosen are obviously in $\mathbb{Q}$.

Core idea

Copy each key in proportion to its probability, then pick one copy uniformly at random.

Proof

Claim. For every rational-valued distribution over a finite key space $\mathcal{K}$ there is a key space $\mathcal{K}'$ such that sampling uniformly from $\mathcal{K}'$ gives the same distribution of keys.

1. Common denominator

By the hint, all key probabilities are rational. Bring them to a common denominator $N$:

$$\Pr[K = k] = \frac{n_k}{N}, \qquad n_k \in \mathbb{N}_0, \qquad \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} n_k = N.$$

2. Copy the keys

Construct $\mathcal{K}'$ by taking $n_k$ copies of every key $k$:

$$\mathcal{K}' := \{(k, j) \mid k \in \mathcal{K},\ 1 \le j \le n_k\}, \qquad |\mathcal{K}'| = N.$$

Let $\mathsf{Gen}'$ sample $(k, j)$ uniformly from $\mathcal{K}'$, and let $\mathsf{Enc}$ and $\mathsf{Dec}$ simply ignore $j$.

3. Same distribution

For every $k \in \mathcal{K}$:

$$\Pr[\mathsf{Gen}' \text{ outputs a copy of } k] = \sum_{j=1}^{n_k} \frac{1}{N} = \frac{n_k}{N} = \Pr[K = k].$$
Result. The key distribution is the same, so the scheme behaves the same. Hence we may assume that $\mathsf{Gen}$ chooses keys uniformly. $\blacksquare$

Example

Let $\Pr[K=a] = \frac12$, $\Pr[K=b] = \frac13$, $\Pr[K=c] = \frac16$. Then $N = 6$, $n_a = 3$, $n_b = 2$, $n_c = 1$, and

$$\mathcal{K}' = \{a_1, a_2, a_3, b_1, b_2, c_1\},$$

where each key is chosen with probability $\frac16$. So $a$ is chosen with probability $\frac36 = \frac12$, $b$ with $\frac26 = \frac13$, and $c$ with $\frac16$.
