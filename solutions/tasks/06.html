Question

Prove that we may assume that the key-generation algorithm $\mathsf{Gen}$ always chooses a key uniformly from the key space $\mathcal{K}$. In other words, for any distribution over $\mathcal{K}$ we can create another key space $\mathcal{K}'$ such that if $\mathsf{Gen}$ samples it uniformly, the distribution of keys is the same.

Hint: the probabilities with which keys are chosen are obviously in $\mathbb{Q}$.

Proof: copy each key in proportion to its probability

By the hint, all key probabilities are rational. Bring them to a common denominator $N$:

$$\Pr[K = k] = \frac{n_k}{N}, \qquad n_k \in \mathbb{N}_0, \qquad \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} n_k = N.$$

Let $\mathcal{K}' := \{(k, j) \mid k \in \mathcal{K},\ 1 \le j \le n_k\}$, so $|\mathcal{K}'| = N$. $\mathsf{Gen}'$ samples $(k, j)$ uniformly from $\mathcal{K}'$, and $\mathsf{Enc}$, $\mathsf{Dec}$ ignore $j$. Then for every $k$:

$$\Pr[\mathsf{Gen}' \text{ outputs a copy of } k] = \frac{n_k}{N} = \Pr[K = k].$$

The key distribution, and hence the scheme's behaviour, is unchanged. $\blacksquare$

Example: probabilities $\frac12, \frac13, \frac16$ for $a, b, c$ give $N = 6$ and $\mathcal{K}' = \{a_1, a_2, a_3, b_1, b_2, c_1\}$. Without the rationality assumption, let the key be $\mathsf{Gen}$'s uniform random tape $\omega$ itself, and compute $k := \mathsf{Gen}(\omega)$ inside $\mathsf{Enc}$ and $\mathsf{Dec}$.
