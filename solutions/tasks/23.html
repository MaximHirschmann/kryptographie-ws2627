Question

Let $\Pi'$ and $\Pi''$ be two private-key encryption schemes for which it is known that at least one of them is CPA-secure. However, you do not know which are the CPA-secure ones. Construct a CPA-secure encryption scheme $\Pi$, which uses $\Pi', \Pi''$, while not knowing which of the two is CPA-secure. Prove that your $\Pi$ is CPA-secure.

Hint: Generate two plaintext messages from the original plaintext so that knowledge of either one of the parts reveals nothing about the plaintext, but knowledge of both does yield the original plaintext.

It is sufficient to give half the proof, that is, to give a proof assuming that $\Pi'$ is CPA-secure. Note that, to complete the proof, one would have to show also that the construction is CPA-secure if one assumes that $\Pi''$ is CPA-secure.

Construction: secret-share the message

Split $m$ into the XOR shares $r$ and $r \oplus m$; each share alone is uniformly random. Encrypt one share with each scheme.

Gen$k' \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}'(1^n)$, $k'' \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}''(1^n)$
Enc$r \leftarrow \{0,1\}^{|m|}$; output $\langle \mathsf{Enc}'_{k'}(r),\ \mathsf{Enc}''_{k''}(r \oplus m) \rangle$
Decoutput $\mathsf{Dec}'_{k'}(c') \oplus \mathsf{Dec}''_{k''}(c'')$

Proof (assuming $\Pi'$ is CPA-secure)

Given an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ against $\Pi$, build $\mathcal{A}'$ against $\Pi'$. It generates $k''$ itself.

  • Oracle queries $m$: choose $r$, get $\mathsf{Enc}'_{k'}(r)$ from its own oracle, compute $\mathsf{Enc}''_{k''}(r \oplus m)$, and return both.
  • Challenge $m_0, m_1$: choose a uniform $s$ and submit $(s \oplus m_0,\ s \oplus m_1)$ to its own challenger. On receiving $c'^* = \mathsf{Enc}'_{k'}(s \oplus m_b)$, give $\mathcal{A}$ the pair $\langle c'^*, \mathsf{Enc}''_{k''}(s) \rangle$.
  • Output $\mathcal{A}$'s guess.

The challenge is a real encryption of $m_b$ with the uniform share $r := s \oplus m_b$ (then $r \oplus m_b = s$). So $\mathcal{A}$'s view is exactly as in its own game, and $\Pr[\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{cpa}}_{\mathcal{A},\Pi} = 1] = \Pr[\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{cpa}}_{\mathcal{A}',\Pi'} = 1] \le \frac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n)$. $\blacksquare$

The case "$\Pi''$ is CPA-secure" is symmetric. The lecture (00_header.pdf, Theorem 1) instead submits the pair $(r, 0^n)$ in Case 1; both proofs are valid.
