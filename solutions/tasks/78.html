Question

Let $N = pq$ for distinct primes $p, q$ and $ed \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(N)}$. The book shows $(x^e)^d \equiv x \pmod N$ for $x \in \mathbb{Z}_N^*$. Show that this holds for all $x \in \mathbb{Z}_N$, including those with $\gcd(x, N) \ne 1$.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 7.10 (restated). Hint: Chinese remainder theorem.

By the CRT it suffices to show $x^{ed} \equiv x$ modulo $p$ and modulo $q$.

Modulo $p$. $\phi(N) = (p-1)(q-1)$, so $ed \equiv 1 \pmod{p-1}$ as well. If $p \nmid x$, Fermat's little theorem gives $x^{ed} = x^{[ed \bmod (p-1)]} = x \pmod p$. If $p \mid x$, both sides are $0 \bmod p$. So $x^{ed} \equiv x \pmod p$ for every $x$.

Modulo $q$. The same argument.

Hence $x^{ed} \equiv x$ modulo $p$ and modulo $q$, and by the CRT modulo $N = pq$, for all $x \in \mathbb{Z}_N$. $\blacksquare$
