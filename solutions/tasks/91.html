Question

Textbook Rabin signatures work like textbook RSA signatures, but with the Rabin function $x \mapsto x^2 \bmod N$: the signer, who knows the factorization $N = pq$, signs a quadratic residue $m$ by outputting a square root $\sigma$ of $m$ modulo $N$; verification checks $\sigma^2 \equiv m \pmod N$. Show that an adaptive chosen-message attack recovers the private key.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 12.3 (restated).

Attack

1.Choose $x \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_N^*$ and ask for a signature on $m := x^2 \bmod N$.
2.Receive $\sigma$ with $\sigma^2 \equiv m$. If $\sigma \not\equiv \pm x$, output $p = \gcd(x - \sigma, N)$ and $q = N/p$.
3.Otherwise repeat with a new $x$.

Why it works

$m$ has four square roots $\pm x, \pm x'$. If $\sigma \not\equiv \pm x$, then $N \mid (x - \sigma)(x + \sigma)$ but $N$ divides neither factor, so $\gcd(x - \sigma, N)$ is $p$ or $q$. The signer sees only $m$, so its root is independent of which of the four roots we picked: $\sigma \notin \{\pm x\}$ with probability $\frac12$. After an expected $2$ queries the attacker knows $p, q$, i.e. the private key. $\blacksquare$
