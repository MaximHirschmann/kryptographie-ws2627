Question

Let $\Pi = (\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Enc}, \mathsf{Dec})$ be a scheme with indistinguishable encryptions in the presence of an eavesdropper (Definition 3.8), where $\mathsf{Enc}_k$ for $k \in \{0,1\}^n$ only accepts messages of length at most $\ell(n)$ for a polynomial $\ell$. Construct a scheme that satisfies Definition 3.8 even if the adversary in $\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}$ may output two messages of different lengths.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 3.4 (restated). This is the positive counterpart of Question 26.

Idea. Pad every message to the same length before encrypting, so the length leaks nothing. Padding must be reversible: append a $1$ and then zeros.

Construction

For messages of length at most $\ell(n) - 1$:

$$\mathsf{pad}(m) = m \,\|\, 1 \,\|\, 0^{\ell(n) - |m| - 1}, \qquad |\mathsf{pad}(m)| = \ell(n).$$
Gen'$k \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$
Enc'$\mathsf{Enc}_k(\mathsf{pad}(m))$
Dec'decrypt, then remove the trailing zeros and the last $1$

Proof

Given an adversary $\mathcal{A}'$ against $\Pi'$ that outputs $m_0, m_1$ of any lengths, let $\mathcal{A}$ output $\mathsf{pad}(m_0), \mathsf{pad}(m_1)$ to its own challenger. These have equal length $\ell(n)$, so the pair is legal for $\Pi$. $\mathcal{A}$ passes the challenge to $\mathcal{A}'$ and outputs its guess. The challenge is distributed exactly like $\mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_b)$, so

$$\Pr[\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}',\Pi'}(n) = 1] = \Pr[\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A},\Pi}(n) = 1] \le \tfrac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n). \qquad \blacksquare$$

The padding costs one bit of message length. (The book's solution pads to $\ell(n) + 1$ bits, which $\Pi$ cannot encrypt.) To keep messages of length exactly $\ell(n)$, pad to $2\ell(n)$ bits and encrypt the two halves under two independent keys.
