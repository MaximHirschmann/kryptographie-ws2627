Question

Lamport's one-time signature scheme is only secure for one signature. Describe an adversary that obtains signatures on two messages of its choice and can then forge a signature on any message.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 12.7 (restated).

Recall Lamport's scheme for $m \in \{0,1\}^\ell$: $sk = (x_{i,b})$, $pk = (f(x_{i,b}))$, and the signature on $m$ is $(x_{1,m_1}, \dots, x_{\ell,m_\ell})$, one secret value per position.

Attack

1.Ask for the signature on $0^\ell$: this reveals $x_{1,0}, \dots, x_{\ell,0}$.
2.Ask for the signature on $1^\ell$: this reveals $x_{1,1}, \dots, x_{\ell,1}$.
3.Now the whole private key is known. For any other $m^*$ output $(x_{1,m^*_1}, \dots, x_{\ell,m^*_\ell})$.

This is exactly the honest signature on $m^*$, so it verifies, and $m^*$ was not queried. The forgery succeeds with probability $1$. $\blacksquare$
