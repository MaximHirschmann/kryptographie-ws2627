Question

A deterministic public-key encryption scheme is used to encrypt a message $m$ that is known to come from a small set of $L$ possible values. Show how to find $m$ in time linear in $L$ (one encryption takes one unit of time).

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 10.2 (restated).

Attack

Let $S = \{m_1, \dots, m_L\}$ be the set of possible messages and $c = \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(m)$.

For$i = 1, \dots, L$: compute $c_i := \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(m_i)$; if $c_i = c$, output $m_i$.

Time: $L$ encryptions and $L$ comparisons, i.e. $O(L)$.

Correctness: since $\mathsf{Enc}$ is deterministic, encrypting the true $m$ gives exactly $c$, so the loop finds it. No other $m_j \ne m$ can give $c$: by correctness $\mathsf{Dec}_{sk}(c)$ has only one value, so a deterministic $\mathsf{Enc}_{pk}$ is injective. The answer is unique. $\blacksquare$

Examples

  • A vote "yes"/"no" ($L = 2$): two encryptions reveal it.
  • A salary in whole euros up to 1,000,000: at most $10^6$ encryptions, a few seconds of work.
  • Textbook RSA $c = m^e \bmod N$ is deterministic, so it is vulnerable in exactly this way.
Result. With a deterministic public-key scheme the adversary can "encrypt and compare" every candidate, which recovers $m$ in time $O(L)$. Hence a deterministic public-key scheme can never be CPA-secure (or even EAV-secure in the public-key setting): choose $m_0 \ne m_1$, encrypt both and compare with the challenge. Public-key encryption must be randomized. $\blacksquare$
