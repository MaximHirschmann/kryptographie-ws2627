Question

Recall Construction 10.12 of a hybrid encryption scheme. Let $\Pi = (\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Enc}, \mathsf{Dec})$ be a public-key encryption system, and let $\Pi' = (\mathsf{Gen}', \mathsf{Enc}', \mathsf{Dec}')$ be a private-key encryption scheme. Construct $\Pi^{hy}$ as follows:

  • $\mathsf{Gen}^{hy}$: on input $1^n$ run $\mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$ and use the public and private keys $(pk, sk)$ that are output.
  • $\mathsf{Enc}^{hy}$: on input $pk$ and $m \in \{0,1\}^*$: choose a random $k \leftarrow \{0,1\}^n$; compute $c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k)$ and $c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m)$; output $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
  • $\mathsf{Dec}^{hy}$: on input $sk$ and $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$: compute $k := \mathsf{Dec}_{sk}(c_1)$; output $m := \mathsf{Dec}'_k(c_2)$.

Theorem 10.13 says that if $\Pi$ is CPA-secure and $\Pi'$ has indistinguishable encryptions in the presence of an eavesdropper, then $\Pi^{hy}$ is CPA-secure. The proof proceeds in three stages:

$$ \begin{array}{ccc} \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle & \overset{\text{"transitivity"}}{\longleftrightarrow} & \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_1) \rangle \\ \updownarrow \text{ case 1, } \mathcal{A}_1 \text{ (security of } \Pi) & & \updownarrow \text{ case 2, } \mathcal{A}_2 \text{ (security of } \Pi) \\ \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle & \underset{\text{case 3, } \mathcal{A}' \text{ (security of } \Pi')}{\longleftrightarrow} & \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_1) \rangle \end{array} $$

The proof fixes an arbitrary PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ in $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}^{hy},\Pi^{hy}}(n)$. Give a formal reduction of cases 1 and 3 by completing the schemas (boxes numbered 1–10: outer box = the reduction, inner box = $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$, boxes on the left = the challenger of the reduction's own experiment).

Left column: the reduction's own challenger. Middle: the reduction (boxes 2 and 7 are internal steps). Right: $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$, run as a subroutine. Keys $k$ are uniform $n$-bit strings.

Case 1: $\mathcal{A}_1$ attacks $\Pi$ in $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}_1,\Pi}(n)$

$\mathcal{A}_1$ uses its challenge to encrypt either the real key $k$ or $0^n$.

#WhereContent
1Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}_1$$(pk, sk) \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$; send $pk$.
2$\mathcal{A}_1$Choose $k \leftarrow \{0,1\}^n$.
3$\mathcal{A}_1 \to$ challengerOutput $k_0 := k$ and $k_1 := 0^n$.
4Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}_1$$b \leftarrow \{0,1\}$; send $c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k_b)$.
5$\mathcal{A}_1 \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Run $\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk)$.
6$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}_1$Receive $m_0, m_1$.
7$\mathcal{A}_1$$c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0)$ (always $m_0$).
8$\mathcal{A}_1 \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Send $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
9$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}_1$Receive a bit $b''$.
10$\mathcal{A}_1 \to$ challengerOutput $b' := b''$.

For $b = 0$, $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ sees the top-left tuple $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle$; for $b = 1$, the bottom-left tuple. So any difference in $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$'s behaviour on these tuples is an advantage for $\mathcal{A}_1$ against $\Pi$, hence negligible. Case 2 is the same with $m_1$ in step 7.

Case 3: $\mathcal{A}'$ attacks $\Pi'$ in $\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}',\Pi'}(n)$

Now $k$ stays inside the challenger. $\mathcal{A}'$ does not need it, because the public-key part only encrypts $0^n$.

#WhereContent
1Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}'$$k \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}'(1^n)$ (secret); start $\mathcal{A}'(1^n)$.
2$\mathcal{A}'$$(pk, sk) \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$.
3$\mathcal{A}' \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Run $\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk)$.
4$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}'$Receive $m_0, m_1$.
5$\mathcal{A}' \to$ challengerOutput $m_0, m_1$.
6Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}'$$b \leftarrow \{0,1\}$; send $c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_b)$.
7$\mathcal{A}'$$c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n)$.
8$\mathcal{A}' \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Send $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
9$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}'$Receive a bit $b''$.
10$\mathcal{A}' \to$ challengerOutput $b' := b''$.

$\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ sees exactly the bottom-row tuple for $m_b$, so $\mathcal{A}'$ wins exactly when $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ guesses $b$, which happens with probability at most $\frac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n)$ by EAV security of $\Pi'$.

Putting it together

Going top-left $\to$ bottom-left (case 1) $\to$ bottom-right (case 3) $\to$ top-right (case 2) changes $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$'s output distribution by at most three negligible amounts, so $\Pr[\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}^{hy},\Pi^{hy}}(n) = 1] \le \frac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n)$. For public-key schemes this is CPA security. $\blacksquare$
