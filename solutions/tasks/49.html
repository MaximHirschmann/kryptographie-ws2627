Question

Recall Construction 10.12 of a hybrid encryption scheme. Let $\Pi = (\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{Enc}, \mathsf{Dec})$ be a public-key encryption system, and let $\Pi' = (\mathsf{Gen}', \mathsf{Enc}', \mathsf{Dec}')$ be a private-key encryption scheme. Construct $\Pi^{hy}$ as follows:

  • $\mathsf{Gen}^{hy}$: on input $1^n$ run $\mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$ and use the public and private keys $(pk, sk)$ that are output.
  • $\mathsf{Enc}^{hy}$: on input $pk$ and $m \in \{0,1\}^*$: choose a random $k \leftarrow \{0,1\}^n$; compute $c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k)$ and $c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m)$; output $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
  • $\mathsf{Dec}^{hy}$: on input $sk$ and $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$: compute $k := \mathsf{Dec}_{sk}(c_1)$; output $m := \mathsf{Dec}'_k(c_2)$.

Theorem 10.13 says that if $\Pi$ is CPA-secure and $\Pi'$ has indistinguishable encryptions in the presence of an eavesdropper, then $\Pi^{hy}$ is CPA-secure. The proof proceeds in three stages:

$$ \begin{array}{ccc} \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle & \overset{\text{"transitivity"}}{\longleftrightarrow} & \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_1) \rangle \\ \updownarrow \text{ case 1, } \mathcal{A}_1 \text{ (security of } \Pi) & & \updownarrow \text{ case 2, } \mathcal{A}_2 \text{ (security of } \Pi) \\ \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle & \underset{\text{case 3, } \mathcal{A}' \text{ (security of } \Pi')}{\longleftrightarrow} & \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_1) \rangle \end{array} $$

The proof fixes an arbitrary PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ in $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}^{hy},\Pi^{hy}}(n)$. Give a formal reduction of cases 1 and 3 by completing the schemas (boxes numbered 1–10: outer box = the reduction, inner box = $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$, boxes on the left = the challenger of the reduction's own experiment).

How to read the schemas. The left column is the reduction's own challenger: $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}$ for $\Pi$ in case 1, $\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}$ for $\Pi'$ in case 3. The middle is the reduction itself; boxes 2 and 7 are its internal computations. The right column is $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$, which the reduction runs as a subroutine. Steps are in the order 1 to 10.

Case 1: $\mathcal{A}_1$ attacks $\Pi$ in $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}_1,\Pi}(n)$

Goal: show $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle \approx \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle$. $\mathcal{A}_1$ uses its challenge to encrypt either the real key $k$ or $0^n$.

#WhereContent
1Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}_1$Challenger runs $(pk, sk) \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$ and gives $pk$ to $\mathcal{A}_1$.
2$\mathcal{A}_1$ (internal)Choose $k \leftarrow \{0,1\}^n$ uniformly.
3$\mathcal{A}_1 \to$ challengerOutput the two messages $k_0 := k$ and $k_1 := 0^n$ (same length $n$).
4Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}_1$Challenger picks $b \leftarrow \{0,1\}$ and sends $c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k_b)$, i.e. $\mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k)$ or $\mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n)$.
5$\mathcal{A}_1 \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Run $\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk)$.
6$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}_1$$\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs two messages $m_0, m_1$.
7$\mathcal{A}_1$ (internal)Compute $c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0)$, always with $m_0$.
8$\mathcal{A}_1 \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Give the challenge ciphertext $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
9$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}_1$$\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs a bit $b''$.
10$\mathcal{A}_1 \to$ challengerOutput $b' := b''$.

Analysis. If $b = 0$, $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ sees exactly $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle$, a real $\Pi^{hy}$-encryption of $m_0$. If $b = 1$, it sees $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle$. Hence

$$ \begin{aligned} \Pr[\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}_1,\Pi}(n) = 1] &= \tfrac12 \Pr\big[\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0)) = 0\big] \\ &\quad + \tfrac12 \Pr\big[\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0)) = 1\big] \\ &= \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 \big(p_{\text{top}} - p_{\text{bottom}}\big) \ \le\ \tfrac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n), \end{aligned} $$

where $p_{\text{top}}$ and $p_{\text{bottom}}$ are the probabilities that $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs $0$ on the top-left and bottom-left tuple. So $p_{\text{top}} - p_{\text{bottom}} \le 2\,\mathsf{negl}(n)$. The variant of $\mathcal{A}_1$ that flips $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$'s output gives the same bound for $p_{\text{bottom}} - p_{\text{top}}$. Case 2 is identical, with $m_1$ instead of $m_0$ in step 7.

Case 3: $\mathcal{A}'$ attacks $\Pi'$ in $\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}',\Pi'}(n)$

Goal: show $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_0) \rangle \approx \langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_1) \rangle$. Now the private key $k$ is hidden inside the challenger. $\mathcal{A}'$ does not need it, because the public-key part only encrypts $0^n$.

1Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}'$Challenger runs $k \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}'(1^n)$ (kept secret) and starts $\mathcal{A}'(1^n)$.
2$\mathcal{A}'$ (internal)Run $(pk, sk) \leftarrow \mathsf{Gen}(1^n)$ itself.
3$\mathcal{A}' \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Run $\mathcal{A}^{hy}(pk)$.
4$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}'$$\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs $m_0, m_1$.
5$\mathcal{A}' \to$ challengerOutput the same pair $m_0, m_1$.
6Challenger $\to \mathcal{A}'$Challenger picks $b \leftarrow \{0,1\}$ and sends $c_2 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_b)$.
7$\mathcal{A}'$ (internal)Compute $c_1 \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n)$.
8$\mathcal{A}' \to \mathcal{A}^{hy}$Give the challenge ciphertext $\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle$.
9$\mathcal{A}^{hy} \to \mathcal{A}'$$\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs a bit $b''$.
10$\mathcal{A}' \to$ challengerOutput $b' := b''$.

Analysis. $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ sees exactly $\langle pk, \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n), \mathsf{Enc}'_k(m_b) \rangle$ with $\mathcal{A}'$'s challenge bit $b$, so $\mathcal{A}'$ wins exactly when $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ guesses $b$:

$$\Pr\big[\mathcal{A}^{hy} \text{ guesses } b \text{ in the bottom row}\big] = \Pr[\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}',\Pi'}(n) = 1] \le \tfrac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n).$$

Putting it together

In $\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}^{hy},\Pi^{hy}}$, $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ sees the top-left tuple if $b = 0$ and the top-right tuple if $b = 1$. Going top-left $\to$ bottom-left (case 1) $\to$ bottom-right (case 3) $\to$ top-right (case 2) changes the probability that $\mathcal{A}^{hy}$ outputs $0$ by at most $\mathsf{negl}_1 + \mathsf{negl}_3 + \mathsf{negl}_2$. This is the "transitivity", and gives

$$\Pr[\mathsf{PubK}^{\mathsf{eav}}_{\mathcal{A}^{hy},\Pi^{hy}}(n) = 1] \le \tfrac12 + \mathsf{negl}(n).$$

For public-key schemes, EAV security implies CPA security (Question 50), so $\Pi^{hy}$ is CPA-secure. $\blacksquare$

Why the detour via $0^n$? The scheme $\Pi'$ only needs EAV security for a key that is uniform and independent of everything the adversary sees. In the top row, $\mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k)$ depends on $k$. After replacing it by $\mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(0^n)$, the key $k$ is used only once, inside $\mathsf{Enc}'$, exactly as in $\mathsf{PrivK}^{\mathsf{eav}}$.
