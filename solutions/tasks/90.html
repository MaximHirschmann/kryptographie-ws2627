Question

Textbook RSA signatures: $\sigma = [m^d \bmod N]$, valid iff $\sigma^e \equiv m \pmod N$. Decide for each of two weaker security notions whether textbook RSA satisfies it, and prove your answer:

  1. The adversary gets $pk$ and a random message $m$, may ask for one signature on a message $m' \ne m$ of its choice, and must then output a valid signature on $m$.
  2. As (a), but the adversary may not ask for any signature.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 12.2 (restated).

(a) One signing query: insecure

Use that RSA signatures are multiplicative.

1.Choose $r \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_N^* \setminus \{1\}$ and ask for the signature $\sigma'$ on $m' := m \cdot r^e \bmod N$. (Then $m' \ne m$.)
2.Output $\sigma := \sigma' / r \bmod N$.

Check: $\sigma^e = (\sigma')^e / r^e = m r^e / r^e = m \pmod N$. The forgery always succeeds. $\blacksquare$

(b) No queries: secure under the RSA assumption

Signing a uniform message $m$ without help means computing $m^{1/e} \bmod N$, which is exactly the RSA problem on a random instance. Formally, an RSA inverter given $(N, e, y)$ hands $pk = (N, e)$ and the message $m := y$ to the forger and outputs its signature. It succeeds whenever the forger does, so the forger's success probability is negligible. $\blacksquare$
