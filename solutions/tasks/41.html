Question

Let $N = pq$ the product of two distinct primes. Give a polynomial algorithm which takes as input $N$ and $\varphi(N)$ and computes $p$ and $q$.

Idea

$\varphi(N)$ and $N$ give the sum and the product of $p$ and $q$. Two numbers with a known sum and product are the roots of a quadratic equation. By 07_number_theory, Lemma 2.1:

$$\varphi(N) = (p-1)(q-1) = pq - p - q + 1 = N - (p + q) + 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S := p + q = N - \varphi(N) + 1.$$

With $pq = N$, the numbers $p$ and $q$ are the roots of

$$X^2 - S X + N = (X - p)(X - q) = 0.$$

Algorithm

1.$S := N - \varphi(N) + 1$
2.$D := S^2 - 4N$  ($= (p - q)^2$, a perfect square)
3.$d := \sqrt{D}$ (integer square root)
4.output $p := \frac{S + d}{2}$, $q := \frac{S - d}{2}$

Correctness: $S^2 - 4N = (p + q)^2 - 4pq = (p - q)^2$, so $d = |p - q|$, and $\frac{S \pm d}{2}$ gives $p$ and $q$.

Running time: with $\|N\| = \log N$ bits, steps 1, 2 and 4 are additions and multiplications of $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$-bit numbers. The integer square root in step 3 can be computed by binary search, or Newton's method, in $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ iterations, each a multiplication. In total this is polynomial in $\log N$, e.g. $\mathcal{O}(\log^3 N)$ bit operations.

Example

$N = 77$, $\varphi(N) = 60$: $S = 77 - 60 + 1 = 18$, $D = 324 - 308 = 16$, $d = 4$, so $p = 11$, $q = 7$.

Consequence: for RSA, computing $\varphi(N)$ is exactly as hard as factoring $N$. Knowing $\varphi(N)$ is enough to factor.
