Question

Let $F$ be a pseudorandom function. Show that the following MAC for messages of length $2n$ is insecure: The shared key is a random $k \in \{0,1\}^n$. To authenticate a message $m_1 \| m_2$ with $|m_1| = |m_2| = n$, compute the tag $\langle F_k(m_1), F_k(F_k(m_2)) \rangle$.

Attack: mix and match

As in Question 34, each half of the tag depends on only one half of the message.

1.Query $x \| y$ and receive $t = \langle F_k(x), F_k(F_k(y)) \rangle$.
2.Query $x' \| y'$ with $x' \ne x$, $y' \ne y$, and receive $t' = \langle F_k(x'), F_k(F_k(y')) \rangle$.
3.Output the message $x \| y'$ with the tag $\langle F_k(x), F_k(F_k(y')) \rangle$: the first half of $t$ and the second half of $t'$.

This is exactly $\mathsf{Mac}_k(x \| y')$, and $x \| y'$ was never queried. So the forgery succeeds with probability $1$, and the MAC is insecure. $\blacksquare$
