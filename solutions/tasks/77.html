Question

Compute the last two decimal digits of $3^{1000}$ by hand.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 7.5 (restated). Same technique as Question 44.

The last two digits are $[3^{1000} \bmod 100]$.

Method 1: Euler's theorem

$100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2$, so by Question 76

$$\phi(100) = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 = 40.$$

$\gcd(3, 100) = 1$, so $3^{40} \equiv 1 \pmod{100}$. Since $1000 = 25 \cdot 40$:

$$3^{1000} = \left(3^{40}\right)^{25} \equiv 1^{25} = 1 \pmod{100}.$$

Method 2: check by repeated squaring

$$3^5 = 243 \equiv 43, \qquad 3^{10} \equiv 43^2 = 1849 \equiv 49, \qquad 3^{20} \equiv 49^2 = 2401 \equiv 1 \pmod{100}.$$

So already $3^{20} \equiv 1$ (the order of $3$ modulo $100$ is $20$, a divisor of $40$), and $3^{1000} = (3^{20})^{50} \equiv 1$.

Result. $3^{1000} \equiv 1 \pmod{100}$: the last two digits are 01. $\blacksquare$
