Question

Compute the last two decimal digits of $3^{1000}$ by hand.

Source: Katz & Lindell, 1st ed., Exercise 7.5 (restated). Same technique as Question 44.

The last two digits are $[3^{1000} \bmod 100]$. Since $\gcd(3, 100) = 1$ and $\phi(100) = \phi(4)\phi(25) = 2 \cdot 20 = 40$ (Question 76), Euler's theorem lets us reduce the exponent modulo $40$:

$$3^{1000} \equiv 3^{[1000 \bmod 40]} = 3^0 = 1 \pmod{100}.$$
Result. $3^{1000} \equiv 1 \pmod{100}$: the last two digits are 01. $\blacksquare$
